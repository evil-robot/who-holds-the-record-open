    How we scored {{N}} countries

    Eight categories, one score

    Each country gets 0 to 100 in eight categories. The overall score is the weighted average. Access and control carry the most weight.

    {{WEIGHTS}}

    Evidence grade (DTI)

    Every score also carries a grade for the evidence behind it, using the eight dimensions of SuperTruth's Data Trust Index™: provenance (primary sources count most), open availability, recency, completeness, corroboration, verification, breadth of sources, and stability (whether our audit found the same legal situation scored differently elsewhere). DTI was built to grade health records; here it grades our evidence. It never changes a score. Tiers: Platinum 90 and up, Gold 80 to 89, Silver 70 to 79, Bronze 55 to 69. Our own rule on top of DTI: evidence with no primary source (law, government, regulator) is never labelled above Silver, and neither is a country where fewer than 4 of its 8 categories cite one.

    Terms used

    • OECD: The Organisation for Economic Co-operation and Development: 38 mostly high-income countries that publish comparable health statistics.
    • WHO: The World Health Organization, the United Nations agency for health. It publishes country data on health workers and spending.
    • EHDS: The European Health Data Space: an EU law (Regulation 2025/327) that gives people in the EU access to their health data across borders. It applies from March 2027, with patient summaries and e-prescriptions from March 2029.
    • GDPR: The General Data Protection Regulation: the EU law on personal data. Health data is a special category, and people have a right to a copy of their data.
    • HIPAA: The Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act: the US law that gives patients a right to copies of their records and limits how providers and insurers use them.
    • DTI: Data Trust Index: SuperTruth's framework for grading how trustworthy data is. Here it grades the evidence behind each score; it never changes a score.

    About the stories

    Everything in this index comes from public information: laws, government and regulator pages, court decisions and published news reports. We link to every source.

    These are published accounts from people and public bodies about problems with health records, pinned to the country where they happened. The summaries are ours; follow each link to read the original. We do not name the people, we include no one under 18, and we name a hospital or company only where a regulator, court or the organisation itself has stated what happened. Stories do not affect any country's score.

    We searched news, regulator and ombudsman decisions, court rulings, parliamentary records and patient groups, in English and local languages. We did not use social media or forums. These are the accounts we could find and publish under these rules. They are not a sample.

    SuperTruth sells health data verification products. We built this index and chose these stories ourselves; no one paid to be included or left out.
